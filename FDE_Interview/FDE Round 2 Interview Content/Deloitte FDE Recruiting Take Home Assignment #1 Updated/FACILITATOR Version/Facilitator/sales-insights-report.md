## Part 1: The Business

### Who Is Meridian?

Meridian sells B2B software with three products:

- **Core Platform** — the foundational product, present in nearly every deal
- **Analytics Add-on** — a premium analytics module sold on top of the platform
- **Security Module** — a compliance and security layer, the smallest revenue contributor

### Three Market Segments

Deals are structured around three buyer types, each with a distinct price point and sales motion:

| Segment | Avg Deal Size Q1 | Typical Sales Cycle | Revenue Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | $178k | ~85 days | 58.8% |
| Mid-Market | $99k | ~82 days | 32.8% |
| SMB | $46k | ~69 days | 8.3% |

Enterprise generates nearly 6x the per-deal revenue of SMB and takes about 23% longer to close. Despite having equal deal counts (20 each), Enterprise and Mid-Market have very different economics. SMB is small in absolute dollars but meaningful as a proving ground for reps and product-market fit signals.

## Part 5: Advanced Insights

### 1. The Real Marcus Rivera Story

Marcus Rivera finished Q1 at 72.4% of quota — the worst result on the team — but the story is more nuanced than a poor performance.

Marcus lost **four deals** in Q1 worth a combined **$985k**. His total won revenue was $615k. His **lost deal value exceeded his won deal value** — meaning for every dollar he closed, he was losing $1.60 in the same period.

The most damaging was a deal he lost on the **last day of the quarter** (March 31): Constellation Corp, valued at **$450k**, lost to SAP. This single deal is larger than any deal anyone else won in Q1 (the next largest was a $275k Priya Patel win). Had Marcus closed Constellation Corp, his Q1 total would have been **$1,065k — a 125.3% attainment** that would have beaten Priya Patel and made him the top rep on the team.

The $450k loss alone accounts for 52.9% of his entire quota miss.

Loss reasons for Marcus in Q1:
- OPP-009 ($220k): Lost to Salesforce, January
- OPP-008 ($195k): No decision, February
- OPP-010 ($120k): Budget freeze, March
- OPP-079 ($450k): Lost to SAP, March 31

This is a rep who generates significant pipeline — including very large deals — but has struggled to close it. The question for Q2 is whether his large pipeline ($1,125k, best on the team) converts, or whether a pattern is emerging.

### 2. The Hockey Stick Problem

50.7% of Q1 revenue closed in March. This is a structural business risk, not just a cosmetic issue. It means:

- **Forecasting is unreliable for most of the quarter.** You can't know in January whether you'll hit your number.
- **Operations surge at quarter end.** Legal, finance, and support teams face compressed timelines.
- **Slippage is magnified.** A deal that moves from March 31 to April 1 moves from Q1 to Q2 — the same deal in the same state moves an entire quarter of credit.

Tom Bradley's Q1 illustrates this most acutely: 59% of his $538k came from three deals closing in the final four days of the quarter (March 28, 30, 31). If any of those three had slipped to April, he would have missed quota.

### 3. SMB Outperforms Enterprise on Efficiency

Carlos Reyes' two-rep SMB team had the highest collective attainment at **106.6%**, outperforming both David Kim's Enterprise team (101.6%) and Lisa Huang's Mid-Market team (101.8%).

This is notable because SMB reps carry the smallest absolute quotas, operate in the smallest deals, and are among the newest hires. Lisa Park (hired Jan 2023) is the 9th most senior rep on the team but delivered the 2nd highest attainment percentage. Ryan Cole (hired Aug 2023, the newest rep) hit 102.3% in his first full quarter tracked here.

The implication: either the SMB segment is underquotaed relative to market opportunity, or Meridian has a product-market fit advantage in smaller companies that doesn't translate as strongly upmarket.

### 4. Security Module Underperformance

The Security Module generated only **$1,050k** in Q1 revenue — just **17.4%** of total — compared to Core Platform at 45.4% and Analytics at 37.2%. This gap is consistent across all three segments:

| Segment | Core Platform % | Analytics % | Security % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | 47.4% | 38.1% | 14.5% |
| Mid-Market | 43.7% | 34.9% | 21.4% |
| SMB | 37.7% | 40.3% | 21.9% |

Security Module is weakest as a share in Enterprise accounts — the exact customers where security compliance requirements are typically most acute. This is counterintuitive. Possible explanations: it is a newer product without enterprise-grade certifications yet, it requires a separate buying process (security/IT vs. business buyer), or it is priced higher than alternatives.

The SMB and Mid-Market segments actually attach Security Module at higher rates than Enterprise, which may indicate the product is better positioned for compliance-mandated SMB use cases than for enterprise security teams.

### 5. Analytics Add-on Dominates SMB

In the SMB segment, the Analytics Add-on outsells the Core Platform: **$202k vs $189k**. This is the only segment where this reversal happens. It suggests SMB buyers are prioritizing analytics capability when they buy — possibly because they lack internal BI tools and see Meridian's analytics as a competitive advantage, while Enterprise buyers already have analytics infrastructure and buy Core Platform first.

This may be an upsell opportunity: if SMB clients lead with Analytics, there may be a motion to expand those accounts onto Core Platform over time, which would drive expansion revenue.

## Part 6: Unusual Patterns and Watch Items

### 🔴 Marcus Rivera's Lost Value Exceeds Won Value

In Q1, Marcus won $615k but lost $985k. This means that for every successful deal, he walked away from roughly $1.60 in failed ones. No other rep comes close to this ratio. His win rate on resolved Q1 deals is **38.5% by value** (4 won, 4 lost), far below the team average of 77.9%. The critical question heading into Q2: his pipeline is the largest on the team at $1.125M. If his current win rate persists, he'll convert only about $430k of it — putting him significantly below his $850k quota again.

### 🔴 Q2 Coverage Is Below the Safety Threshold

A typical rule of thumb in B2B sales is to maintain **3x pipeline coverage** — meaning $18.6M in pipeline for a $6.2M quota. Meridian's Q2 pipeline sits at $5.2M plus $518k already won, for roughly **0.9x coverage**. This is workable only if:
(a) Win rates are significantly above industry average, or
(b) Substantial new pipeline is created before quarter end

Given Q1's late-closing pattern, (b) is likely — but it means Q2 will again be decided in the final weeks of June.

### 🔴 Tom Bradley's Thin Q2 Pipeline

Tom Bradley ended Q1 strong (107.6%) and started Q2 strong ($293k won, 56% of quota in two weeks). But his open Q2 pipeline is only **$220k** — barely enough to cover his remaining $227k. He has almost no margin for error. He either needs to close virtually every open deal, or he needs to be actively building new pipeline right now. If any of his three open deals slip to Q3, he will miss Q2.

### 🟡 Kevin Marsh Gets a Raise Despite Missing

Kevin missed Q1 at 90.4% — a shortfall of $50k — yet received a Q2 quota increase to $540k (+3.8%). This is unusual. The most charitable interpretation is that management views his losses (one to an incumbent, one to budget freeze) as market-driven rather than rep-driven. The riskier interpretation is that his quota was raised before anyone examined why he missed. If Kevin misses Q2 as well, a pattern is established.

### 🟡 Aisha Williams Has Weakest Q2 Pipeline Coverage

Despite a strong Q1 (108.2%), Aisha enters Q2 with the lowest pipeline-to-remaining-quota ratio at **57%**. She has three open deals ($80k Negotiation, $90k Proposal, $70k Negotiation) against a $420k remaining quota. Even if she closes all three at full value, she would be at roughly 48% of Q2 quota. She needs meaningful new deals to enter and close within Q2 — possible, but it requires active prospecting in an already thin pipeline environment.

### 🟡 Priya Patel's Q2 Bar Just Got Higher

Priya crushed Q1 at 120.8% and was rewarded with a $1M Q2 quota — a 5.3% raise that takes her above every other rep. Her Q2 pipeline is $740k in 4 deals (3 in Negotiation, including a $280k Sterling Analytics deal). If she closes all three Negotiation deals, she reaches roughly $765k — still $235k short. She will need to generate and close additional pipeline within Q2 to maintain her top performer status.

### 🟡 Budget Freezes Clustering Late in Q1

All four deals lost to budget freezes occurred in February and March — none in January. This could be coincidence, or it could indicate an external trend (companies freezing discretionary spend mid-quarter). If it's a pattern, the same dynamic may resurface in Q2's February–March equivalent (May–June), which is exactly when the bulk of Q2 deals are projected to close.

### 🟡 The "No Decision" Problem

Three Q1 deals worth $318k closed as "No decision" — meaning the prospect evaluated Meridian and chose not to buy anything. These are different from competitive losses (where someone bought a competitor's product) — they represent deals where the business case was not strong enough to move forward at all. "No decision" losses are often solvable with better qualification: if a rep identifies early that a prospect lacks budget authority or business urgency, time can be redirected to higher-probability deals. With $318k lost to indecision, this is worth examining in the sales process.

### 🟢 SMB May Be Underquotaed

Carlos Reyes' two-rep team (Lisa Park + Ryan Cole) beat quota as a team by 6.6%, with both reps finishing above their individual numbers. Their combined quota of $470k is by far the smallest of the three teams. If the SMB segment has real market depth, the company may be leaving revenue on the table by capping these reps' targets. Lisa Park in particular — with 7 deals, the most of any rep, at an 87% win rate — looks like a rep who is limited by the number of accounts she can reach rather than by her ability to close.
